A movie in action

The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt follows Geralt of Rivia, a witcher for hire, as he travels across a war-torn land searching for his adopted daughter, Ciri. What begins as a personal rescue quickly grows into one of the most emotionally deep and complex stories ever told in a video game.

What makes the story stand out is how real it feels. There are no clear heroes or villains, just people making tough choices in messy situations. Every character feels human, with motives you can understand even when you don’t agree. The game doesn’t tell you what’s right or wrong, it simply puts you in hard situations and lets you live with the choices you make.

The side quests are just as strong as the main story. What seems like a simple task often turns into a full story with its own twists, consequences, and memorable characters. CD Projekt Red gave every part of the game the same level of care, and it clearly shows.

The cast

Geralt is one of gaming's most compelling protagonists, not because he is flashy or overpowered, but because he is tired. He is a man who has seen too much, done too much, and carries the weight of a world that largely despises him for what he is. Yet he keeps going, keeps helping, keeps making hard calls. That quiet humanity is what makes him so easy to root for.

The supporting cast is equally strong. A few standouts:

  • Ciri — trying to survive while carrying a burden she never chose
  • Yennefer — driven and complex, often hiding more than she shows
  • Dandelion — lighthearted on the surface, but loyal and dependable when it matters
  • The Bloody Baron — deeply flawed, with a past that's hard to ignore

These are not characters who exist to serve the plot. They have their own lives, their own agendas, and their own stories that play out regardless of what Geralt does. That sense of a living world full of real people is something very few games manage to pull off.

A World Alive With Detail

The world of The Witcher 3 is huge, but what matters more than its size is how full of life it feels. Every village has its own story. Every notice board offers contracts that pull you into local problems. Every tavern has a gwent player ready to challenge you. The world doesn’t feel made for a game, it feels like a place that existed before you arrived and will keep going after you leave.

The environments themselves are breathtaking. Velen is dark and muddy, soaked in war and suffering. Novigrad is a busy city full of merchants, criminals, and politics. Skellige is wild and windswept, all cliffs and Norse-inspired culture. Each region has its own identity, its own soundtrack, and its own challenges for Geralt to face.

The weather, the day and night cycle, and how NPCs react to Geralt based on his location and reputation all combine to make the world feel alive. Entering a new town and hearing people whisper about the witcher never gets old.
